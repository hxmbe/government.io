Practice Questions

1. Which best explains the regional variation in gun ownership shown in the data?

(A) The Second Amendment applies differently in different states, giving some regions broader constitutional protections.
(B) Differences in state laws, rural vs. urban population density, cultural traditions around hunting and self-defense, and regional political identity contribute to the variation.
(C) Federal background check requirements are enforced more strictly in the Northeast, suppressing ownership rates there.
(D) McDonald v. Chicago (2010) applied Second Amendment protections only to rural areas where hunting is common.

2. Based on the data, which conclusion about gun policy and federalism is best supported?

(A) Because gun ownership is uniform across regions, federal regulations will have an equal impact on all states.
(B) Wide regional variation suggests one-size-fits-all federal gun legislation may face stronger opposition in high-ownership regions, creating pressure to leave regulation to the states.
(C) States with lower ownership rates have uniformly enacted stricter gun laws as a result of public pressure.
(D) The federal government cannot regulate firearms under the Supreme Court's current interpretation of the Second Amendment.
