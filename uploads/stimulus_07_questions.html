1. Which best describes the trend in Supreme Court decision-making from the 1980s to the present?

(A) The Court has become more divided, with 5–4 decisions rising while unanimous decisions decline.
(B) Unanimous decisions have increased while the 5–4 margin has declined, suggesting broader consensus on most argued cases.
(C) The distribution of vote margins has remained essentially stable, suggesting political factors have little effect.
(D) The rise in unanimous decisions reflects a chief justice strategy to avoid the appearance of partisanship.

2. Critics who argue 5–4 decisions undermine Court legitimacy would most likely cite which argument?

(A) Decisions split along partisan lines suggest justices behave like political actors rather than neutral legal interpreters, eroding public trust.
(B) The Constitution requires supermajority agreement among justices before any ruling takes effect.
(C) A 5–4 decision gives the president authority to override the ruling by executive order.
(D) Congressional approval is required before a 5–4 decision can become binding precedent.
