Practice Questions

1. Which best explains the positive correlation between household income and voter turnout?

(A) Federal law requires proof of income as part of voter registration, making wealthier citizens more likely to register.
(B) Higher-income citizens tend to have more flexible work schedules, greater access to transportation, higher educational attainment, and stronger political efficacy — all of which increase likelihood of voting.
(C) Wealthier Americans are constitutionally guaranteed an additional vote in federal elections because they pay higher taxes.
(D) Lower-income citizens are more likely to live in states with restrictive voter ID laws that disproportionately prevent them from voting.

2. A political scientist analyzing the income-turnout gap would most likely argue it contributes to which outcome?

(A) Because higher-income citizens are more moderate, their disproportionate turnout produces more centrist election outcomes.
(B) The income-based turnout gap means wealthier Americans' preferences are overrepresented in election outcomes, potentially biasing policy toward higher-income constituents.
(C) Lower turnout among low-income voters does not affect policy since both parties target the same high-income donor class.
(D) Wealthier voters consistently support expanding social safety net programs, so their overrepresentation benefits lower-income Americans.
Answer Key  ·  Q1 → B    Q2 → B
